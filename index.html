INNOVATION OVERVIEW & EARLY PROTOTYPE

TextileMap

The challenge

Textile sourcing involves many suppliers, materials, production processes and sustainability factors.

Relevant information may be distributed across several reports, systems and data sources, which makes it difficult to create a clear overview and compare alternatives.

This becomes particularly important when sourcing decisions need to consider environmental indicators such as emissions and water usage together with operational and supplier-related information.

The concept

TextileMap is a dashboard concept that brings selected supply-chain information together in one interface.

The intention is to support sourcing employees by making supplier, material and sustainability information easier to access, compare and investigate.

TextileMap is intended as a decision-support tool. It should support professional judgement rather than replace it.

Target users

Employees working with material sourcing.

Main need

A clearer overview of complex supplier, material and sustainability information.

Information explored

Supplier locations, materials, CO₂ emissions, water usage, renewable energy, labor-related information, certifications and supply risks.

Intended value

Easier access to relevant information when comparing sourcing alternatives.

DESIGN THINKING PERSPECTIVE

User value, feasibility and viability

USER VALUE · USABILITY

Intended users

The primary users are employees working with material sourcing.

TextileMap addresses the difficulty of maintaining an overview when sourcing decisions depend on information from multiple suppliers, materials and systems.

The interface is designed to provide a high-level overview first while still allowing users to investigate details when needed.

TECHNICAL REALIZATION · FEASIBILITY

Data and implementation

A real implementation would require access to reliable supplier, material and sustainability data.

Relevant data may include emissions, water usage, renewable energy, manufacturing information, geographical information and climate-related risks.

Integration with relevant IKEA systems and consistent methods for keeping data current would be important technical requirements.

BUSINESS ALIGNMENT · VIABILITY

IKEA context

TextileMap could support sourcing work by making sustainability and supply-chain information easier to access during decision-making.

The concept is aligned with the need to consider sustainability alongside other sourcing factors.

Its long-term usefulness depends on whether the information is trustworthy, maintainable and compatible with existing sourcing workflows.

SUSTAINABLE SOFTWARE REQUIREMENTS

Sustainability analysis

The Sustainable Requirements Framework is used to evaluate possible effects of TextileMap across the individual, social, environmental, economic and technical dimensions. The analysis considers immediate, enabling and structural effects.

Individual

Easier access to information may improve users' understanding of sourcing alternatives and support professional agency. At the same time, excessive reliance on a dashboard could reduce autonomy if its recommendations are treated as authoritative.

Health · lifelong learning · privacy · safety · agency

Social

A shared information source may improve collaboration and transparency between people working with sourcing. Trust can be damaged if supplier information is inaccurate, incomplete or difficult to verify.

Community · trust · inclusiveness · equity · participation

Environmental

Making information such as emissions, water usage and supply-chain risks easier to compare could support sourcing decisions with lower environmental impact. The software itself also requires computing resources, storage and hardware.

Materials · waste · energy · biodiversity · logistics

Economic

Improved access to decision-relevant information may create value by supporting sourcing decisions. Development, integration, maintenance and continuous verification of supplier data also create costs.

Value · CRM · supply chains · governance · innovation

Technical

TextileMap needs to remain maintainable, secure and adaptable as suppliers, materials, data sources and sustainability requirements change.

Maintainability · usability · adaptability · security · scalability

Example chain of effects
