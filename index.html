CAPSTONE PROJECT · TEAM 11

TextileMap

TextileMap is an early-stage software concept that explores how textile supply-chain information could be presented in a clearer and more comparable way for material sourcing employees at IKEA.

Project focus Textile supply chains
Primary users Material sourcing employees
Current phase Research & early prototyping
Team Team 11
01

INNOVATION OVERVIEW

Problem and concept

The challenge

Textile sourcing involves many suppliers, materials, production processes and sustainability factors. Relevant information may be distributed across several reports, systems and data sources, which makes it difficult to create a clear overview and compare alternatives.

This becomes particularly important when sourcing decisions need to consider environmental indicators such as emissions and water usage together with operational and supplier-related information.

The concept

TextileMap is a dashboard concept that brings selected supply-chain information together in one interface. The intention is to support sourcing employees by making important information easier to access, compare and investigate.

The system is intended as a decision-support tool. It should not replace professional judgement, and the reliability and comparability of the underlying data remain central concerns.

Target users

Employees working with material sourcing.

Main need

A clearer overview of complex supplier, material and sustainability information.

Possible indicators

CO₂ emissions, water usage, renewable energy, labor score, certifications and supply risk.

Expected value

More accessible information that can support informed and potentially more sustainable sourcing decisions.

02

USER RESEARCH

How the concept has been shaped so far

Interviews

We have interviewed people with experience in sourcing and supply chains. The interviews provided insight into how difficult it can be to maintain an overview when decisions depend on information from multiple parts of a supply chain.

Desk research

We have also reviewed IKEA sustainability material and sustainability strategy documents to better understand the organizational context and the types of sustainability concerns that may be relevant to the concept.

Planned evaluation

In later stages, the plan is to let potential users interact with the prototype and provide feedback using a think-aloud approach. This can help identify unclear information, missing functionality and usability problems.

Questions guiding the work

  • Which information is most useful when comparing suppliers?
  • How much detail should be visible at first glance?
  • How should uncertainty and data quality be communicated?
  • Which sustainability indicators are meaningful and comparable?
  • How can the interface support rather than replace user judgement?

EARLY PROTOTYPE

Current TextileMap prototype

The prototype explores how geographically distributed suppliers, sustainability indicators and detailed supply-chain information could be combined in one interface. It is currently being used to explore information structure, interaction and functionality.

TextileMap Prototype
Interactive

The prototype above is interactive. It can also be opened separately for a larger view.

Open prototype
01

Geographical overview

Suppliers are positioned on a global map so that the user can understand where different parts of the textile supplier network are located.

02

Supplier sustainability metrics

The prototype presents sustainability-related indicators connected to individual suppliers. These currently include measures such as sustainability score, CO₂ emissions, water usage, renewable energy and labor-related data.

03

Detailed supplier information

Selecting a supplier gives the user access to more detailed information about materials, manufacturing processes, certifications, sustainability performance and identified challenges.

04

Material and process information

The prototype allows users to investigate more than high-level sustainability scores. Supplier-specific material and process information can provide additional context behind the displayed values.

05

Future comparison support

A longer-term goal is to support comparison between suppliers and materials using several relevant indicators. The intention is to support a complex sourcing decision rather than reduce it to a single score.

Prototype data

The current prototype contains supplier names, descriptions and numerical values used for interface development and evaluation. Unless explicitly stated otherwise, these values should not be interpreted as verified information about IKEA's actual supplier network.

DESIGN THINKING PERSPECTIVE

Usability, feasibility and viability

USABILITY

User value

The interface is intended for material sourcing employees who need to understand and compare complex information. A simple overview should be available first, with the option to investigate details when needed.

FEASIBILITY

Technical realization

A real implementation would depend on access to reliable supplier information, emissions and water data, geographical information and integration with relevant IKEA systems or data sources.

VIABILITY

Business alignment

The concept could support sourcing work by making sustainability information more accessible during decisions. Its usefulness depends on whether it fits existing workflows and whether the data can be trusted and maintained.

SUSTAINABLE SOFTWARE REQUIREMENTS

Sustainability dimensions

The Sustainable Requirements Framework is used to consider both positive and negative effects of the concept. The analysis includes immediate effects of the software, behaviors it may enable, and broader structural effects that may develop over time.

Individual

Easier access to information can support learning and user agency. However, users may lose autonomy if the dashboard is treated as an authority rather than as decision support.

Agency · learning · privacy

Social

A common information base may improve collaboration and transparency. Trust can be weakened if data is incomplete, misleading or presented without sufficient context.

Trust · collaboration · participation

Environmental

The concept may support sourcing choices with lower environmental impact by making relevant indicators visible. The digital system itself also requires storage, computing resources and hardware.

Resources · emissions · water · logistics

Economic

Better access to decision-relevant data may create value, while development, integration, maintenance and data-quality work generate ongoing costs.

Value · supply chains · governance

Technical

The system needs to remain maintainable, secure and adaptable as suppliers, materials, data sources and sustainability requirements change.

Maintainability · adaptability · scalability

Example chain of effects

Immediate

Supply-chain and sustainability information is brought into one interface.

→
Enabling

Sourcing employees can compare suppliers and materials more efficiently.

→
Structural

Decision patterns may increasingly favor suppliers with stronger sustainability performance.

RISKS & LIMITATIONS

Important limitations at this stage

Data quality

Supplier data may differ in accuracy, age, scope and measurement method. Poor comparability could result in misleading conclusions.

Transparency

The interface needs to communicate uncertainty and data provenance instead of presenting every value as equally reliable.

User over-reliance

A dashboard can simplify complex decisions too much. The concept should support professional judgement rather than automate it away.

Maintenance

Supplier information, environmental indicators, regulations and risk conditions change over time and need continuous updating.
