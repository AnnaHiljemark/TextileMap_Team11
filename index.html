CAPSTONE PROJECT · TEAM 11

TextileMap

TextileMap is an early-stage software innovation exploring how information about textile supply chains can be presented in a clearer and more comparable way for material sourcing employees at IKEA.

The project focuses on bringing supplier, material and sustainability information together in one interface to support sourcing decisions.

Project focus Textile supply chains
Primary users Material sourcing employees
Current phase Research and early prototyping
University Chalmers University of Technology
01

INNOVATION OVERVIEW

Problem and concept

The challenge

Textile sourcing involves many suppliers, materials, production processes and sustainability factors.

Relevant information can be spread across different reports, systems and data sources. This can make it difficult for sourcing employees to gain an overview and compare different alternatives.

Decisions may also involve several sustainability indicators, such as emissions, water usage and risks connected to different geographical regions.

The TextileMap concept

TextileMap is a dashboard that visualizes supply chains for different textiles and suppliers.

The objective is to collect selected information in one interface and make it easier for users to move between a high-level overview and more detailed information about suppliers, materials and processes.

TextileMap is intended as a decision-support tool. It should support professional judgement rather than replace it.

Target users

Employees working with material sourcing.

Main user need

A clearer overview of complex supplier, material and sustainability information.

Information considered

Supplier information, materials, CO₂ emissions, water usage, renewable energy, climate-related risks and other sustainability indicators.

Intended value

Making relevant information easier to access and compare when making sourcing decisions.

02

USER RESEARCH

Understanding the sourcing context

Interviews

We have interviewed people with experience in sourcing and supply chains.

The interviews provided insight into the complexity of keeping track of information across a supply chain and highlighted the need for clear and accessible information when comparing alternatives.

Desk research

To adapt the project to the IKEA context, we have also studied IKEA sustainability reports and sustainability strategy material.

This research has helped us identify sustainability information that may be relevant when evaluating suppliers and materials.

Planned prototype evaluation

During later stages of the project, we plan to let potential users interact with the prototype.

Think-aloud testing can be used to understand how users interpret the interface, which information they find useful and where the design creates uncertainty.

Questions guiding our work

  • Which information is most useful when comparing suppliers?
  • How much information should be visible in the first overview?
  • When does a user need more detailed information?
  • How should uncertainty and data quality be communicated?
  • Which sustainability indicators can be meaningfully compared?
  • How can the system support professional judgement without replacing it?
03

EARLY PROTOTYPE

Current TextileMap prototype

Our current prototype explores how supplier locations, sustainability indicators and detailed supply-chain information could be presented within the same system.

The prototype is still under development. Its purpose is currently to explore information structure, navigation and functionality rather than represent a finished IKEA system.

01

Global supplier map

Suppliers are displayed geographically, allowing users to see where suppliers and different parts of the textile supply chain are located.

02

Sustainability information

The prototype includes sustainability indicators connected to individual suppliers.

Examples currently explored include CO₂ emissions, water usage, renewable energy and other supplier-related metrics.

03

Supplier details

Selecting a supplier provides access to more detailed information.

The interface can present information about materials, manufacturing processes, sustainability performance and other relevant supplier characteristics.

04

Comparison

An important future function is the ability to compare different suppliers and materials using several indicators rather than relying on a single value.

Prototype data

The prototype currently contains information and numerical values used to demonstrate and evaluate the interface.

Unless explicitly stated otherwise, these values should not be interpreted as verified information about IKEA's actual suppliers.

DESIGN THINKING PERSPECTIVE

Usability, feasibility and viability

USER VALUE / USABILITY

Intended users

The primary users are employees working with material sourcing.

TextileMap aims to reduce the effort required to find and compare relevant information from different parts of a supply chain.

The interface should provide a simple overview while still allowing users to investigate more detailed information when needed.

TECHNICAL REALIZATION / FEASIBILITY

Data and implementation

A future implementation would require access to reliable supplier and material data.

Relevant data may include emissions, water usage, manufacturing information, geographical data and climate-related risks.

Integration with existing IKEA data sources would also be an important technical consideration.

BUSINESS ALIGNMENT / VIABILITY

IKEA context

TextileMap is intended to support sourcing work by making sustainability and supply-chain information more accessible during decision-making.

The concept may support IKEA's sustainability work if it can provide relevant, reliable and maintainable information within existing sourcing workflows.

SUSTAINABLE SOFTWARE REQUIREMENTS

Sustainability analysis

We use the Sustainable Requirements Framework to evaluate possible effects of TextileMap across five sustainability dimensions.

The analysis considers immediate effects of the software itself, enabling effects created by how the software is used and possible structural effects over a longer period of time.

Individual

Easier access to information may improve users' ability to understand and compare supply-chain alternatives.

However, if employees rely too heavily on the dashboard, the system could reduce professional autonomy rather than increase it.

Agency · Learning · Privacy
Social

A shared information source may improve communication and collaboration between employees working with sourcing.

Trust may be weakened if supplier data is inaccurate, incomplete or presented without sufficient transparency.

Trust · Collaboration · Transparency
Environmental

Making environmental information more accessible could support sourcing decisions with lower environmental impact.

At the same time, the software requires computing resources, storage and hardware throughout its lifecycle.

Resources · Energy · Logistics
Economic

Better access to relevant information may improve sourcing decisions and create business value.

Development, integration, maintenance and continuous verification of data all require resources.

Value · Supply chains · Governance
Technical

TextileMap needs to remain maintainable and adaptable as suppliers, materials and sustainability requirements change.

Scalability, security and data quality are therefore important long-term technical requirements.

Maintainability · Security · Scalability

Example chain of effects

IMMEDIATE

Supply-chain and sustainability information is collected and visualized within one interface.

→
ENABLING

Material sourcing employees can compare suppliers and materials more efficiently.

→
STRUCTURAL

Sourcing decisions may increasingly reward supply chains with stronger sustainability performance.

RISKS AND LIMITATIONS

Current challenges

Data quality

Reliable and comparable data across many different supply chains may be difficult to obtain.

Comparability

Sustainability indicators may use different measurement methods, boundaries and data quality standards.

False confidence

A clear interface can make uncertain information appear more reliable than it actually is.

Maintenance

Supplier information, regulations, sustainability requirements and climate risks change over time and require continuous updating.
